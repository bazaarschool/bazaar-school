With a future you agree today on the price of a deal that completes on expiry day. You do not pay the full value. You post a margin, typically 10 to 15 percent of the contract, and the exchange settles gains and losses in cash every evening. Index futures settle in cash; stock futures held to expiry end in delivery of the shares.

The margin makes futures leveraged. A small move in the index is a large move on the money you put up, in both directions, and a sharp move can cost more than your whole margin.

### Example

You buy one Nifty future at 25,000. With a lot size of 65 the contract is worth about 16.25 lakh rupees, against a margin of about 2 lakh. If the Nifty rises 300 points you gain 19,500 rupees. If it falls 300 points you lose the same.

### Watch out

SEBI found that about nine in ten individual traders in futures and options lost money over three recent years. Futures are tools for hedging and professional trading, not a faster way to invest.
