ROE tells you how hard the owners' money is working. A company with a 20 percent ROE that keeps its profit could, in principle, grow its book value 20 percent a year without raising new capital. In India an ROE above 15 percent held for many years is a good sign, and above 25 percent for a decade is rare.

ROE has one big blind spot: debt. Borrowing adds assets without adding equity, so a company can lift its ROE simply by borrowing more. Screeners split ROE into profit margin, asset turnover and leverage (the DuPont breakdown) so you can see where it comes from.

### Example

A company earns a profit of 300 crore rupees on shareholders' equity of 1,500 crore. Its ROE is 20 percent.

### Watch out

Two companies with the same 20 percent ROE can be very different animals. One earns it with fat margins; the other borrows five rupees for every rupee of equity. Look at the debt before you admire the number.
