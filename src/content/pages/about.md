## Why this exists

Most people meet the market through a tip. A cousin's WhatsApp forward, a YouTube thumbnail with a rocket on it, a colleague who "doubled his money" in a stock whose business he cannot describe. The basics come later, if at all, and usually after a loss.

We wanted the opposite order. Start with what a share actually is. Then what Sensex and Nifty measure. Then how a demat account works and what happens between tapping "Buy" and owning something. Only after that, how to judge a company, and only after *that*, the instruments and rules that cost people real money.

## How the site is arranged

**Bazaar Basics, Intermediate and Advanced** are courses. Each is a numbered list of lessons, and each level assumes you finished the one before it. You can skip around, but the numbering is the point.

**Bazaar Stories** is history: the scams, crashes, rituals and people that made Dalal Street what it is. The market makes more sense once you know where it came from.

**Bazaar AI** looks at the tools everyone is suddenly using. What a language model is good at (reading a 300-page annual report so you do not have to), what it is bad at (picking tomorrow's winner), and what SEBI lets a retail investor do with algorithms.

## Who writes it

Lessons are written and reviewed by a small editorial team. We read the primary sources: SEBI circulars, exchange rules, the Finance Act, company filings. Where a number matters, we say where it came from and when it was written. Where we are unsure, we say that too.

We are not SEBI-registered investment advisers or research analysts. That is not false modesty; it is a legal line, and it shapes everything here. We explain how things work. We do not tell you what to buy.

## What we will never do

- Publish buy or sell calls, price targets or "multibagger" lists.
- Run a paid Telegram or WhatsApp group.
- Let an affiliate partner decide what a lesson says. Some links on the site are affiliate links and are marked as such; the lesson would read the same without them.
- Pretend that reading a website replaces a registered adviser when your situation calls for one.
